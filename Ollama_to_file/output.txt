The Earth is round (or more precisely, approximately spherical) primarily due to gravity and its own rotation.

Here's why:

1.  **Gravity Pulls Equally in All Directions:** Gravity acts on every particle of mass in the Earth. This force pulls matter towards the center of mass. For a large enough body like Earth, this gravitational pull is strong enough that it draws all the material together toward the geometric center.
2.  **The Sphere is the Natural Shape:** When you have a collection of particles (like dust or liquid) all attracted equally to a central point by gravity, they naturally form the shape with the greatest volume for their given mass – which is a sphere. This state is called hydrostatic equilibrium (for fluids like molten rock early in Earth's history) or self-gravity equilibrium.
3.  **Pressure and Weight:** Imagine packing material into a roughly spherical shape. The weight of everything above pulls down on the lower layers, creating immense pressure that compresses them further towards the center. This process continues until all parts are pulled as close to the center as possible by gravity, resulting in a near-perfect sphere.

So, gravity gathered the matter and compressed it into the most stable shape – a ball or sphere. While Earth rotates slightly (which causes its equator to bulge out a bit more than the poles), making it an oblate spheroid, this rotation effect is much smaller compared to the overall spherical shape caused by gravity.

Essentially, any large celestial body with sufficient mass and gravity will tend to form into a sphere because that's the most gravitationally stable configuration.
